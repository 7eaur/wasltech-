# Wasl Social Poster & Campaign System

## Purpose

Use this skill to turn a brand's services and positioning into a reusable visual-and-marketing system. Produce a family of clean poster templates, concise Arabic copy, campaign sequences, weekly publishing plans, and conversion paths that grow relevant followers and generate qualified leads.

Do not treat every poster as a standalone artwork. Build a repeatable system: **fixed brand rules + flexible layouts + one message per asset + a clear campaign role**.

## Inputs to collect

Before designing or planning, identify:

- Brand name, logo, colors, fonts, website, phone/WhatsApp, and target platforms.
- Exact services and the business outcomes each service supports.
- Primary audience, geography, language, and level of awareness.
- Main commercial goal: followers, leads, sales, bookings, or a defined combination.
- Available proof: real projects, testimonials, numbers, screenshots, before/after examples.
- Whether the asset is organic, paid, awareness, education, trust, or conversion content.
- Required aspect ratio and whether the user needs editable source files or only final images.

If information is missing but low-risk, make a reasonable assumption and label it. Do not invent proof, prices, customer results, or guarantees.

## Core positioning

For digital-service brands, avoid leading with a flat list of services. Frame the offer as a transformation:

> **Understand the client's problem first, then choose the digital solution that fits.**

For brands like Wasl Tech, the solution family may include websites, company profiles, mobile/web apps, e-commerce stores, custom digital solutions, digital identity, and turning an idea into a real product. Present these as different paths within one journey, not as unrelated services.

## Research before campaign planning

When the user asks for a strong, researched campaign:

1. Inspect the brand's official site and extract actual services, audience clues, value propositions, process, proof, and contact details.
2. Research 3–5 relevant competitors or substitutes in the target market.
3. For each competitor, note services, positioning, recurring content angles, visible strengths, missing proof, unclear pricing/scope, and opportunities for differentiation.
4. Synthesize patterns rather than copying language or visuals.
5. Build the campaign around a gap: clearer scope, stronger proof, more practical education, better local relevance, or a more specific customer problem.

Keep competitor observations factual and respectful. Never attack competitors or claim superiority without evidence.

## Campaign architecture

Build two parallel objectives:

### Follower-growth track

Use painful, relatable, and useful content that earns follows, saves, and shares. The viewer should think: **“This is about my project.”**

### Lead-generation track

Use service explanations, proof, process, and conversion posts that direct the right viewer to WhatsApp, a form, a consultation, or a defined next step.

Use this sequence:

> **Pain/awareness → useful education → solution fit → proof/process → conversion**

Do not make every post sell. A strong campaign has awareness posts without a large CTA, while conversion assets use a specific CTA.

## Five-day weekly publishing model

Use five main publishing days when the user wants an active campaign:

| Day | Asset | Primary role |
|---|---|---|
| Saturday | Pain-led static poster | Stop scrolling and attract relevant followers |
| Sunday | Educational carousel | Earn saves and shares |
| Monday | Service/solution poster | Explain one service through one outcome |
| Tuesday | Trust/process/comparison carousel | Reduce uncertainty and build belief |
| Wednesday | Paid conversion poster | Generate qualified WhatsApp conversations |

Use stories for polls, questions, behind-the-scenes, proof, reminders, and retargeting. Reels are optional; if excluded, do not replace them with cluttered static posters—use carousels and strong single-image assets instead.

## Hook system

Create at least three hook options for every main post. Hooks must be short, specific, and painful without using false fear. Prefer customer language over company language.

Useful formulas:

- **Loss:** “كم عميل ضاع لأنك تأخرت؟”
- **Competitor pressure:** “منافسك يبيع الآن… وأنت ما زلت تخطط.”
- **Clarity gap:** “مشروعك ممتاز… لكن هل يظهر كذلك؟”
- **Decision friction:** “إذا احتار… راح لغيرك.”
- **Idea delay:** “فكرتك عالقة في رأسك؟”
- **Speed:** “في عصر السرعة، التأخير قرار.”
- **Problem-first:** “لا تبدأ بالتقنية… ابدأ بالمشكلة.”
- **Service fit:** “موقع؟ تطبيق؟ متجر؟ ابدأ من احتياجك.”

For every hook, supply:

- Primary hook.
- Two or three alternatives.
- One short supporting line, usually 3–6 words.
- CTA only when the post's role requires conversion.

## Visual design system

Do not use one rigid layout for every post. Create 4–6 related templates with the same visual language:

1. Pain and hook.
2. Idea to reality.
3. Service/product visual.
4. Educational carousel.
5. Comparison or transformation.
6. Paid conversion.

### Shared visual rules

- Use one clear message and one focal point.
- Prefer clean white, light-gray, or restrained brand-color backgrounds.
- Place the logo consistently, normally in the upper-right for Arabic layouts.
- Keep a quiet contact strip at the bottom when appropriate: phone/WhatsApp and domain.
- Use a large central visual, but do not force devices into every design.
- Choose the visual metaphor that explains the message: storefront, roadmap, sketch, product box, customer journey, organized workspace, identity elements, or a single relevant mockup.
- Keep supporting copy short. Put detailed explanation in the caption or carousel.
- Use one or two fonts, a restrained palette, generous margins, and strong contrast.
- Avoid unnecessary glow, particles, neon effects, dramatic lens flares, random lines, excessive reflections, or decorative clutter.
- Do not let AI generate the final brand logo, Arabic copy, phone number, domain, or legally important details when exactness matters. Prefer controlled placement/editing for those elements.

### CTA rule

Not every post needs an in-artwork CTA.

Use no large CTA button for awareness, pain, questions, educational tips, and follower-growth assets. A quiet contact strip may remain.

Use a clear CTA for paid ads, service offers, idea-to-reality posts, evaluations, consultations, or lead-generation assets. Make the CTA specific:

- “أرسل كلمة ابدأ”
- “شاركنا فكرتك”
- “اطلب تقييم موقعك”
- “ابدأ متجرك”
- “ناقش حل مشروعك”

Avoid repeating generic “تواصل معنا” when a more useful action is available.

## Production workflow

1. Write the one-sentence job of the asset: audience + desired action.
2. Select the campaign stage and template family.
3. Write three or more hook options.
4. Select one short supporting line and decide whether a CTA is needed.
5. Choose one visual metaphor or one central composition.
6. Create or source the visual asset; use AI for concept imagery when useful, but assemble exact brand elements with controlled design tools.
7. Compose the poster using the brand grid: logo, hook, supporting line, central visual, contact strip, optional CTA.
8. Review Arabic text, punctuation, spelling, logo, phone number, domain, contrast, and mobile readability.
9. Produce variants only when the objective changes; do not create superficial variants.
10. For campaigns, map each asset to a day, objective, audience, CTA, and measurement event.

## Visual review checklist

Score each criterion from 1–10 and explain any score below 8:

- Hook understood in two seconds.
- One clear focal point.
- Supporting copy is short and useful.
- Background is clean and non-distracting.
- Logo is consistent and secondary to the message.
- Arabic typography is readable and correctly shaped.
- Hierarchy is obvious: hook → visual proof → next step.
- Contact strip is legible but not dominant.
- CTA is absent when unnecessary and specific when needed.
- Visual metaphor matches the service/problem.
- Design works at mobile-feed size.
- Series consistency is visible without making every post identical.

## Measurement and optimization

Track follower and lead outcomes separately.

### Follower outcomes

- Relevant followers gained.
- Profile visits.
- Saves and shares.
- Comments revealing a real pain point.
- Cost per engaged or qualified follower when promoted.

### Lead outcomes

- WhatsApp conversations by keyword.
- Qualified conversations.
- Cost per qualified conversation.
- Requests for quotes, consultations, or project briefs.
- Conversion by service type.

Do not optimize solely for likes. Test one or two variables at a time: hook, visual metaphor, audience, or CTA. Keep the strongest organic posts as candidates for paid promotion.

## Deliverables

When asked to create a campaign, deliver:

- Core campaign idea and positioning.
- Audience and pain points.
- Competitor-derived differentiation, when research is requested.
- Weekly publishing table.
- Post-by-post concept, hook options, supporting copy, visual direction, CTA, objective, and audience.
- Story prompts and WhatsApp flow.
- Paid/organic split and budget logic.
- Measurement plan.

When asked to create posters, deliver final images plus a concise review. When editable source is requested, use the appropriate design tool or editable format; do not claim an AI-generated PNG is editable.

## Quality bar

The final system should feel like one brand with multiple voices: **clean, fast to understand, emotionally relevant, visually disciplined, and commercially useful**. Use the reference brand as a benchmark for consistency and clarity, never as a source to copy.
